## Advisor responses

### Contrarian (Response B)
The preset's biggest operational lie is the Outsider boundary, and a real user will get it wrong. Step 1A says "gather domain context... but NOT for the Outsider" — but the council mechanism spawns sub-agents, and this is a reference doc dropped into a framing step, not enforced code. Nothing here tells the configuring engineer HOW to withhold context from one seat while feeding it to four others. The user reads "but NOT for the Outsider," nods, and pastes the whole bundle into a shared frame anyway. The seat that's supposed to catch the onboarding cliff now silently holds the system in its head, and the review loses its single most distinctive signal — quietly, with no error.

Second, the Watch-item contradicts the council's "report, don't prescribe" rule and the artifact never reconciles it. It instructs the adjudication layer to "keep both and note whether they reinforce or diverge" — fine — but the Contrarian skin is told to argue "the simpler shape that would have avoided this" and First-Principles "don't build this." Two seats are now both prescribing a verdict, not reporting observations.

Third, "self-hostable infrastructure over managed-service convenience" is baked into the audience descriptor as a default. That's a thumb on the scale. Any review run from this preset is pre-biased against the managed-service answer before the Contrarian opens its mouth — the author smuggled a conclusion into the scope line and called it context.

### First-Principles (Response A)
The Executor seat is dead weight, and the preset's own logic exposes it. The real test of every seat is: what does it catch that no other seat catches? Run that test on the Executor. Its flagship catch — *elegant on paper, no realistic path to production* — is already caught twice: First-Principles says the elegance solves the wrong problem, Contrarian says it won't survive contact with 3am. "Migration path" and "ship incrementally" aren't a thinking stance; they're a checklist. A checklist doesn't need a seat at a deliberative table — it needs a line in the output template.

Notice where the preset itself admits this. The "Watch-item" section is a confession: Contrarian and First-Principles "will often both say don't build this." The author flagged the overlap on the wrong pair. The genuine redundancy is Executor ↔ the don't-build-this consensus: when two seats say stop and one seat's only counter is "but here's the smallest step," that third seat isn't a tension-holder, it's a tie-breaker the adjudication layer should own. You wrote "KEEP for engineering" in bold — that emphasis is the tell. Ask the prior question: does operability-and-shipping need a thinking stance, or a rubric?

### Expansionist (Response D)
The strongest thread is the "Watch-item specific to engineering" — buried at the bottom as a footnote. Look at what it encodes: a rule for distinguishing convergent agreement (two seats happen to land on the same verdict) from compositional signal (two independent failure modes stacking). "Reinforce = strong signal to stop; diverge = two separate problems" is a reusable adjudication primitive, not an engineering-specific quirk. It belongs to the council's core thesis ("developmental, not convergent") — it is the mechanism by which surfaced tension stays informative instead of collapsing into a vote count.

The adjacent leverage: this watch-item generalizes to every preset. The life-decision sibling has the same risk. If you lift this from "engineering watch-item" to a named pattern in the council core (call it agreement-disambiguation, or reinforce-vs-diverge), every domain skin inherits it.

The second undervalued asset is the reversible-vs-one-way-door descriptor in the scope layer. You note it "strongly affects what counts as in-scope" and move on. That dial deserves to be load-bearing: irreversibility is precisely when the council's developmental stance pays off most. Develop that link explicitly — it is the strongest argument for why this tool beats a straight answer.

### Outsider (Response C — bare artifact, zero enrichment)
The document in front of me is itself a configuration for the council — a meta-document. Things that confuse me or read as assumed knowledge:
- "developmental council," "seats," "framing step," "adjudication layer," "Step 1A," "tension-pairs," "skin" — all used as if I know them. "Skin" especially is never defined.
- "ADRs" appears once with no expansion (inferring Architecture Decision Records).
- "one-way-door" / "reversible-vs-irreversible" — used as a known concept.
- "CLAUDE.md" is referenced as a thing that exists and matters; no idea what it is from this page.
- "operability" treated as obviously meaningful, contrasted with throughput; distinction assumed, not drawn.
- The seats are named as if a canonical roster exists elsewhere. "KEEP for engineering" implies some seats get dropped in other configs — but the default set is never stated here.
- "Drop this context into the framing step, or keep it as a reference" — two modes named, neither explained.

What doesn't add up: the doc tells the Outsider to read with no system knowledge, yet to even occupy this seat I must already understand the whole council apparatus. The instructions presuppose the very insider context the seat is supposed to lack. The strongest, clearest part: the Watch-item — stands on its own without outside context.

### Executor (Response E)
The Outsider carve-out is the place execution breaks. The preset says "Step 1A — but NOT for the Outsider," but the seat configuration spawns five sub-agents from one framing step. Nothing tells the operator HOW to withhold context from one seat while feeding the other four. Monday morning: do I spawn the Outsider with a different prompt? A redacted artifact? An AI assistant running the skill will almost certainly hand all five the same context bundle because that's the path of least resistance. The differentiator silently collapses.

Second stuck-point: "the relevant code / configs / manifests for the component in question" has no realistic first step for a large system. Which files? The preset offers no heuristic (blast radius? files touched by the change? the diff?). With a real codebase someone either dumps everything or guesses, and both poison the review.

Third: the audience descriptor has a fill-in-the-blank — "[reversible / one-way-door]" — flagged as strongly affecting scope, but never says who decides or when. That choice needs to be a required input, not a bracketed suggestion. The seat list is otherwise immediately actionable.

## Gap pass (3 reviewers, "what did everyone miss?")

**Reviewer 1:** All five treat the preset as the unit of analysis; none questioned whether "engineering review" should be a preset at all — yet their own observations dismantle nearly every engineering-specific element (D moves Watch-item + reversibility to core, A deletes Executor, B removes the self-hostable bias, C says seats imply a generic roster). Subtract all that and almost nothing domain-specific remains. Two pairs reveal more together: B and E hit the Outsider carve-out as an execution gap, but only C proves the deeper contradiction — the seat can't be both context-starved AND know enough to hold a council seat; the mechanism B/E want is impossible as specified. And A vs. D are in direct tension no one saw: A says overlapping seats are redundant (kill Executor); D says overlap is the product (reinforce-vs-diverge is signal). The council must decide whether seat redundancy is waste or compositional value.

**Reviewer 2:** A and D both touched the Watch-item but pulled opposite ways and neither saw the connection: the reinforce/diverge logic is exactly the test that proves or kills A's redundancy claim. If reinforcing is signal not waste (D), then A's "every seat must catch something unique" premise is wrong. The council has no stated theory of when redundancy is signal versus dead weight. Second: B, C, and E independently hit the Outsider but missed the paradox C alone glimpsed — you cannot operationally starve one seat AND have it understand the apparatus enough to occupy it. The Outsider isn't underspecified — it's incoherent as a same-context seat; it must be a separate pass, not a seat.

**Reviewer 3:** Every advisor judged the preset as if it were the skill. A/E demand the Outsider carve-out have an enforcement mechanic; B calls it "an operational lie." But a preset only owes its reader a worked example of seat-skinning — the apparatus (how to withhold context, the default roster, "report don't prescribe") is owned by SKILL.md, which they never opened. No one read the sibling life-decision preset, which answers nearly every complaint: it names when to skip, models a sparing context rule, and its Watch-item holds the guardrail line ("never set aside for being hard to hear"). The connection no one drew: A (Executor is dead weight) and D (Watch-item is the real value) are the same observation inverted — both say the preset's worth is in its adjudication logic, not its seat list. And the question a real engineering review most needs — "does this catch the decision we'll regret in six months" — went unasked, as did whether [reversible/one-way-door] belongs in every preset.

This down-weights B's "operational lie," E's "breaks execution," and C's "undefined terms / paradox," and sharpens the genuinely preset-level defects.

### Converge (high-confidence)
1. The Outsider carve-out is the riskiest line (Contrarian, Executor, Outsider) — severity adjusted: the mechanic exists in SKILL.md; the preset's fault is not pointing to it.
2. The Watch-item is the file's center of gravity (Expansionist as strongest idea, Contrarian as possible rule-conflict).

### Clash (left standing)
First-Principles (kill the Executor — overlap is waste) ↔ Expansionist (overlap is the product — reinforce/diverge is signal). Both gap reviewers named this as the core unresolved tension. Not resolved here. Input for the author: SKILL.md already makes Executor optional and notes dropping it unpairs First-Principles — so the skill's own heuristic is First-Principles' argument; the preset's bold KEEP is the disputed domain recommendation.

### Bottom line
Well-designed for its job (seat-skinning example) — mostly yes. Missing: a code-bounding heuristic, a required reversibility input, a pointer to Outsider enforcement, and the reinforce-vs-diverge idea surfaced. Misleads in two places: the self-hostable default bias, and the "but NOT for the Outsider" line read standalone (looks like a configurer must hand-build what the skill already does).
